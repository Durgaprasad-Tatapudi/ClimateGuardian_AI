\begin{abstract}
Rising frequencies of climate-driven extreme weather events necessitate robust, multi-hazard early warning systems. However, operational deployment is frequently hindered by complex stateful sequence models and target-proxy data leakage during evaluation. This study proposes ClimateGuardian AI, a hybrid machine learning and deep learning framework for the real-time prediction of flood, heatwave, and compound climate risks. Operating on a 45-feature historical hydro-climatic dataset (2000--2018, 6,940 daily records), the framework engineers temporal and lagged variables to contextualize instantaneous environmental conditions. To ensure rigorous evaluation, we employ strict chronological partitioning and leakage-aware train-only preprocessing. Hazard-specific algorithms include a Random Forest for flood risk ($F_1=0.4686$), a proxy-remediated LightGBM for heatwave risk ($F_1=0.8878$), and a Logistic Regression stacking ensemble combining XGBoost and LightGBM bases for operational compound risk ($F_1=0.3419$). An offline Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) sequence benchmark evaluates temporal capacity ($F_1=0.4343$). Comprehensive evaluation incorporates threshold-independent discrimination, probability calibration, extended lead-time analysis, feature ablation, and SHAP-based interpretability. Finally, the framework demonstrates end-to-end stateless operational inference natively integrated with live Open-Meteo weather data.
\end{abstract}
